Day 6 · Thursday, October 15

Yosemite → Tioga Road → Lake Tahoe

⚠️ The trip's critical road day. Tioga Road is a high mountain pass, and October is right at the edge of its season — if it's closed, this route has to be rebuilt with a lower alternate, not improvised on the fly.

⚠️ Check the road before you leave the hotel. Tioga Road has no fixed closing date — it's snow-triggered and has closed anywhere from mid-October to late November in different years. Check Yosemite's current conditions page or call 209-372-0200 first thing. If it's closed, the route becomes CA-120 back through Manteca/Sonora up to US-395 — plan on significantly more driving time and start earlier.

  2. Shorten Tuolumne Meadows to a drive-through rather than a stop
  3. Keep Olmsted Point, Tioga Pass, and Emerald Bay — the day's three anchor photo stops

🏨 Tonight's stay — South Lake Tahoe

Target $90–120.

Cheaper alternate: Carson City, NV, ~30 min away, is notably cheaper — Nevada-side lodging near Tahoe generally runs lower than the California south shore.

📌 Facts about today

  • Tioga Pass, at 9,943 ft, is the highest highway pass in California and the eastern gateway to Yosemite — it typically opens as late as May or June and can close as early as the first serious autumn storm, which is why checking conditions same-day matters so much here.
  • Mono Lake is more than twice as salty as the ocean and has no outlet — water only leaves by evaporation, which is exactly what built up the tufa towers over thousands of years as calcium-rich spring water met the alkaline lake water.
  • Lake Tahoe is the largest alpine lake in North America and so deep (max ~1,645 ft) that its water never fully freezes, even in the coldest Sierra winters.
