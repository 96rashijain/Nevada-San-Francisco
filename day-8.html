Napa Valley scenic drive Must 📷
Choose CA-29 or the Silverado Trail — don't drive both extensively, you'll cover most of the same views. Rolling vineyard rows in October color are the whole point of this stretch.

Yountville — Should

Small upscale Napa town with restaurants, gardens, and vineyard surroundings on all sides. A good lunch stop. 🍽️ Oxbow Public Market (Napa town, just south of Yountville) — a food hall with multiple vendors including plant-based options at Hudson Greens & Goods; easy to find something for everyone in the same stop.

Yountville, CA

St Helena — Should

Historic Napa Valley town with pretty tree-lined streets and well-preserved early-1900s architecture. 🍽️ Farmstead at Long Meadow Ranch — farm-to-table with seasonal vegetarian dishes.

St Helena, CA

Calistoga — Optional

Farthest north of the towns on this route — the first thing to drop if you're behind schedule.

A winery estate exterior — Optional

You don't need to pay for a tasting to enjoy Napa. Pick one photogenic estate if you're interested in a quick exterior photo stop — no need to pay for a formal tour.

🎒 Carry today

  • Camera — this is one of the most photogenic driving days of the trip
  • Comfortable shoes for walking Yountville/St Helena

🏨 Tonight's stay — Napa

Target $100+ — Napa proper runs higher than most of this trip's stops.

Cheaper alternate: Vallejo or Fairfield, ~20–30 min from Napa town, have notably cheaper chain hotels if you want to keep this night's cost down.

📌 Facts about today

  • Napa Valley has more than 400 wineries packed into a valley roughly 30 miles long and 5 miles wide — one of the highest densities of wineries anywhere in the world.
  • The valley's unusual mix of volcanic, alluvial, and marine sedimentary soils across a small area is part of why it supports so many distinct wine styles in such a compact space.
  • Yountville is named for George Calvert Yount, the first American settler to receive a Mexican land grant in the Napa Valley, in 1836.
