Day 7 · Friday, October 16

Lake Tahoe — west shore

An easy, scenic day after yesterday's long push. One beach, a drive up Highway 89, and back — this is a day to slow down, not collect stops.

One structural note: today's west-shore drive is technically a there-and-back from South Lake Tahoe, and an alternate version of this trip could instead sleep in Tahoe City/West Shore first and approach Napa the next day via I-80 through Truckee instead of doubling back. We kept the South Lake Tahoe-both-nights version because Emerald Bay sits just 15–20 min from South Shore (so the "backtrack" is short), and it avoids extra one-night hotel switching — but if you'd rather restructure it, that's the trade-off.

Vehicle access is closed for the 2026 off-season starting October 5 — camping closed October 3. Not worth building into this trip.

Tahoe's water reads an almost unreal deep teal-to-navy in good light — lake teal and deep water blue are the two colors that photograph best against it. Bring a warm layer even midday; the lake keeps the air noticeably cooler than the valleys you've been in.

📌 Facts about today

  • Lake Tahoe's water is famous for its clarity — visibility has historically been measured to depths of 60–70 feet in the clearest spots, though decades of development have reduced it somewhat from mid-20th-century peaks.
  • Sugar Pine Point State Park's Ehrman Mansion was built in 1903 and is one of the best-preserved examples of the grand lakeside estates wealthy San Franciscans built around Tahoe in the early 1900s.
  • D.L. Bliss State Park is named for Duane L. Bliss, a 19th-century timber and railroad magnate whose logging operations once stripped much of the Tahoe basin's original forest — the park today protects some of the shoreline's last old-growth trees.
