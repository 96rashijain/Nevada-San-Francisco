Day 4 · Tuesday, October 13

Visalia → Yosemite Valley

Tunnel View is the postcard shot, then a loop through the Valley's icons ending at golden hour. Park once and stay parked — don't shuffle the car between every stop.

Mariposa (recommended)
Lodging note — Mariposa over Oakhurst: per NPS's own driving-distance chart, Mariposa is 44 mi / ~1h15m from Yosemite Valley, while Oakhurst's fastest route (via CA-41) is 55 mi / ~1h30m — about 15 min more each way. It's a real gap, not a dramatic one, but since you're visiting the Valley on both Day 4 and Day 5, it's paid twice. Book Mariposa if rates are reasonable; Oakhurst is a perfectly fine fallback if Mariposa is sold out or notably pricier for your dates — it's not worth overpaying to save 30 min a day.

Valley floor stop sequence Must
El Capitan Meadow → Sentinel Bridge → Cook's Meadow → (Lower Yosemite Fall + the Chapel if time allows). See descriptions below.

El Capitan Meadow — Must, Photo, Wildlife

Wide open meadow directly beneath the 3,000 ft granite face of El Capitan. Bring your phone's zoom or binoculars — you can often spot climbers on the wall. Birds are regularly visible over the meadow too.

El Capitan Meadow

Sentinel Bridge — Must, Photo

The classic Half Dome reflection shot, especially near sunset when the water is calm. One of the most reliable "wow" photos of the whole trip.

Sentinel Bridge

Cook's Meadow — Must, Wildlife

Flat open meadow with clean sightlines to Half Dome and Yosemite Falls — also one of the better deer-spotting areas in the Valley, especially near dawn/dusk. Fall foliage adds color in October.

Cook's Meadow

Lower Yosemite Fall — Should

Short, easy walk if water is flowing. If it's dry or weak (common by mid-October), skip it — you won't be missing much.

Yosemite Chapel — Optional

A small historic wooden chapel, the oldest structure in the Valley still in its original location. Worth a quick photo stop if you're passing it, not a special trip.

Swinging Bridge — Optional

A quiet river/granite view a short walk from Sentinel Bridge, with a smaller-crowd feel. Add it only if you're walking past anyway — it's not worth a dedicated trip on this schedule.

5:45–6:30 PM
Valley View Must 📷
One of the best final-light viewpoints of the day — a low, river-level view of the Valley with El Capitan and Bridalveil Fall framing the scene. A strong finish before you leave.

🎒 Carry today

  • Layers — 60s by day, upper 30s once the sun drops behind the walls
  • Binoculars or phone zoom for El Capitan climbers / meadow wildlife
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Parking pass / reservation confirmation if one is required for your travel dates — check ahead

🍽️ Eat

  • Lunch: packed Jain food, or Degnan's Kitchen (Yosemite Village)
  • Dinner: Mariposa has ordinary chain/casual options; if staying Oakhurst, South Gate Brewing Company has a gastropub menu with vegetarian-adaptable dishes — confirm current options directly, it's not a dedicated vegetarian kitchen

➕ Optional enroute

  • Should Bridalveil Fall — worth the short walk even if flow is low
  • Should Lower Yosemite Fall / Yosemite Chapel — quick add-ons only if time allows

If you're behind — cut in this order

  1. Drop the Chapel first
  2. Drop Lower Yosemite Fall next (especially if it's dry)
  3. Keep Tunnel View, El Capitan Meadow, Sentinel Bridge, Cook's Meadow, and Valley View — those are the day's core

🏨 Tonight's stay — Mariposa (recommended)

Target $90–120. Mariposa's real distance advantage (see the note above) makes it worth paying a bit more here rather than optimizing purely for price — you'll feel it on both Day 4 and Day 5.

Fallback: Oakhurst, if Mariposa is sold out or notably pricier — it has more dining options, including South Gate Brewing Company on Highway 41. Cheaper alternate for either: Fresno, about an hour from the Valley, has big-box chain pricing — only worth it if you don't mind the extra drive time both this evening and tomorrow.

📌 Facts about today

  • El Capitan's granite face rises about 3,000 ft from base to summit — roughly twice the height of the Empire State Building — and remains one of the largest single blocks of exposed granite on Earth.
  • Half Dome isn't actually missing half its dome — geologists estimate erosion and glacial activity removed somewhere around 20% of the original rock, not 50%, though the sheared face makes it look far more dramatic than that.
  • The Ahwahneechee people lived in Yosemite Valley for thousands of years before Euro-American contact; many of the park's place names (including "Yosemite" itself, likely from a Miwok-language term) trace back to Indigenous naming.
